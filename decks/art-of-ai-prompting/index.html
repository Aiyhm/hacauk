What is AI, really?

It guesses the next word.

Twinkle, twinkle, little ?star

star 97% lamb 2% car 1%

The capital of France is ?Paris

Paris 99% Lyon <1% London <1%

The best major on campus is BusinessDesignLawBiologyFinanceMediaCSEnglish

??? 9% ??? 9% ??? 8%

Clear prompt, one answer. Vague prompt, a guess.

Finish my sentence (40 seconds, voices only). Read each line, cue the room with your hand, they shout the last word.

  • "Twinkle, twinkle, little…" → one loud answer → click (reveals star).
  • Click (new line). "The capital of France is…" → one loud answer → click (reveals Paris).
  • Click (new line). "The best major on campus is…" → chaos. Let it spin for a few seconds, then click for the punchline.

Punchline: You just did what ChatGPT does: predict the next word from everything you have ever read. When I was clear, you agreed. When I was vague, you guessed. So does it.

This is how chat AI works. Image tools work differently; we get to them later.

Same artists. Better prompt.

Sentence 1 is already up. Read it, then click three times, reading each new sentence slowly. Do not click again: press T for 40 seconds. The next click reveals the answer.

Timer ends → "Hold them up." The room is now full of near-identical houses. Stay silent for five seconds. Then click: this is the house that was in my head the whole time.

Debrief: "After round one, what did you want to ask me?" Take three shouts (what kind, for who, how big).

Punchline: You did not get more talented in 40 seconds. My instructions got better. AI cannot see the picture in my head, and it never gets to ask. The questions you wanted to ask are the prompt.

A R T

…in a frame

Four lines. Then talk back.

Name the four sentences they just obeyed, one click each:

  • Audience — who it is for and why. The context AI cannot see.
  • Role — who the AI should be: tutor, coach, editor, cartoonist.
  • Task — the one exact thing you want made.
  • Frame — the shape of the answer: length, format, tone, limits.

This workshop is The ART of AI Prompting. A, R, T, in a frame.

Last click: a good prompt is not one message. Reply. Push back. Ask for shorter, funnier, harder. Every prompt for the rest of the session starts from these four lines. Watch which ones each tool needs.
